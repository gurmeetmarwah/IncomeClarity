What is credit card APR?

APR is the yearly “price tag” for borrowing on your card when you don’t pay the full balance. It’s shown as one big percentage on your offer and statement—but the bank charges a slice of it every month (or every day) on what you still owe.

In plain English: Pay your full statement balance by the due date and you may owe $0 interest on purchases (when a grace period applies). Leave money on the card and APR turns into real dollars on your bill—the higher the APR and balance, the bigger that charge.

APR in 30 seconds

APR (annual percentage rate) answers: “If I carry a balance all year, roughly how expensive is this card?” It’s not your minimum payment—that’s just the smallest amount to keep the account current.

Think of it like rent on borrowed money

You “rent” the bank’s money when you don’t pay the balance off. APR is the yearly rent rate. The bank doesn’t wait until year-end—they take a little rent each billing cycle on what you still owe.

That’s why paying only the minimum can feel like running uphill: a big chunk of each payment may go to this “rent” (interest) instead of shrinking what you actually borrowed (principal).

Four APR types on most cards

One plastic card can list several APRs. Which one applies depends on what you did—shop, transfer old debt, take cash, or miss payments.

Purchase APR

Everyday spending—groceries, gas, online orders.

This is usually what people mean by “my card’s interest rate.”

Balance transfer APR

Debt you moved from another card.

Often starts with a 0% intro for a few months, then jumps to a normal rate.

Cash advance APR

ATM withdrawals and similar cash-like uses.

Usually the highest rate, often no grace period, plus fees.

Penalty APR

A higher rate after serious late payments.

Not every card uses it—but check your agreement if you’ve been very late.

Your card’s Schumer box (in the mailer or online) lists these numbers. Your monthly statement shows which rate hit which part of your balance.

How APR becomes a charge on your bill

Banks don’t charge the full APR once a year. They use a smaller daily or monthly rate and apply it to the balance that’s allowed to accrue interest during the billing cycle.

Quick example (rounded)

$1,000 balance at 24% APR → about $20 in interest for one month before fees (24% ÷ 12 ≈ 2% × $1,000). Real statements use the issuer’s daily balance method, but this shows why APR matters.

Rates that change, pause, or spike

  • Variable APR — Most US cards move with the economy (often tied to “prime” + a margin). Your rate can go up or down after notice.
  • 0% intro APR — A temporary break on interest for purchases or transfers if you follow the rules. When it ends, the normal rate applies to what’s left.
  • Penalty APR — A higher tier that may kick in after repeated very late payments. Know your card’s rules before you rely on a promo rate.

Use your APR in a payoff plan

Find your purchase APR on the statement (or the rate that actually applies to your revolving balance). Plug balance + APR + monthly payment into the payoff calculator to see months until debt-free and total interest.

Even a 2–3 point APR drop—or $50 more per month—can save hundreds when a balance sits for years.

Frequently asked questions

What does APR mean on a credit card?

It’s the yearly cost of carrying a balance, shown as a percentage. The bank converts it into smaller monthly or daily charges on what you still owe.

What is the difference between purchase APR and cash advance APR?

Purchase APR is for normal spending. Cash advance APR is for cash-like transactions—usually higher, often starts charging right away, and may have extra fees.

How do you calculate monthly interest from APR?

A rough shortcut: APR ÷ 12 × balance (e.g. 24% ÷ 12 = 2% per month on the balance). Your statement uses the bank’s exact formula—this is just to get in the ballpark.

Is APR the same as interest rate?

For credit cards, people use both terms to mean the same thing: how much borrowing costs per year. The dollar charge on your bill is “interest”; APR is how that charge is labeled upfront.

What is a variable APR?

A rate that can change when a benchmark (like the prime rate) moves. Fixed-rate general-purpose cards are rare in the US market.
