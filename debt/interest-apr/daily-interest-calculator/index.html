How daily interest is calculated

APR is a yearly rate. Split it by 365 to get one day’s share. That is your daily periodic rate.

On debt, daily interest is a cost. On savings, it is money you earn. The same math works both ways. Change the principal, rate, or days above and the chart shows how the balance moves.

Daily interest rate

APR ÷ 365

Example: $10,000 at 18% APR → 18% ÷ 365 = 0.0493% per day

Daily interest: $4.93/day (simple, before compounding)

Daily interest on credit cards

Cards use a daily periodic rate. That is APR ÷ 365. Interest hits your balance every day you carry debt.

A $5,000 balance at 24% can cost about $100 a month in interest alone. If you pay only the minimum, most of that payment may go to interest. The balance shrinks slowly.

Seeing the daily number—about $3.29 a day on $5,000 at 24%—makes the leak feel real. That is why paying more than the minimum matters.

Daily interest on savings accounts

APY vs APR: Savings ads often show APY. It includes compounding. APR is the plain yearly rate.

Many high-yield accounts compound daily. Your balance grows a little each day. Pick daily compounding in the calculator for savings.

On $10,000 at 4.5% APY, you might earn about $1.23 a day. It is small per day but adds up over a year.

Frequently asked questions

What is daily interest?

It is the interest that builds in one day. Banks use APR ÷ 365 for the daily rate.

How do banks calculate daily interest?

They multiply your balance by the daily rate. Cards often compound each day. Some loans wait until month-end.

What is a daily periodic rate?

APR divided by 365. At 18% APR, that is about 0.0493% per day.

Is daily interest better than monthly interest?

For debt, daily math usually costs more. For savings, daily compounding can earn more.

How much is 18% APR per day?

On $10,000, about $4.93 per day with simple interest.

Do credit cards charge interest daily?

Yes. If you carry a balance past the grace period, most US cards charge every day.

How do savings accounts earn daily interest?

Banks post interest based on APY. Use daily compounding in the tool for savings estimates.
