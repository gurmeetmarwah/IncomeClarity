What is affordable childcare?

Context matters more than shame—these bands help you frame care costs against gross household income.

Under 10% of income

Comfortable for many families—care is meaningful but not swallowing the budget.

10–20% of income

Moderate burden—common in dual-income households; tighten discretionary spend.

20–30% of income

Financially tight—may require tradeoffs in housing, savings, or work structure.

30%+ of income

High burden—often unsustainable without assistance, family help, or higher income.

Ways to lower childcare costs

Practical levers families use—none replace your own eligibility research for programs.

Shared nanny arrangements

Family care support

Frequently asked questions

How much does childcare cost?

Costs vary by city, age, and care type. Daycare for one toddler often runs $1,000–$1,600/month in mid-cost metros and $1,800–$2,500+ in expensive cities. Nanny care is typically higher.

What income is needed to afford daycare?

Many planners use 10–20% of gross income as a moderate band. At $1,500/month care, that implies roughly $90k–$180k gross depending on your target percentage.

Is childcare cheaper than a nanny?

Usually yes for center or home daycare vs full-time nanny pay. Nannies offer flexibility; daycare offers structure and often lower cost.

How much should childcare cost relative to income?

Under 10% comfortable · 10–20% moderate · 20%+ stressful for many families. Your safe line depends on housing and debt.

What states have affordable childcare?

Lower-cost metros in Texas, Arizona, Georgia, and parts of the South often show lower averages than coastal cities—still compare your income in the planner.
