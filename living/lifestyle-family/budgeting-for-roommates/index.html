How to split rent fairly

Pick a strategy before move-in—money conflicts often start with unclear splits, not bad intentions.

Equal split

Best when bedrooms are identical and everyone uses shared space similarly. Simplest to track; hardest to defend if one room is clearly larger.

Split by bedroom size

Most common in real leases. Master suite pays 10–20% more; shared rooms pay less. Use square footage or agreed weights in the planner.

Split by income

Useful when salaries differ widely. Higher earners cover a slightly larger share so no one is house-poor. Put percentages in writing.

Utility-weighted split

Fair when one person runs AC constantly, has a home office, or uses more bandwidth. Split base rent equally; weight utilities by usage or income.

Roommate compatibility checklist

Budget fairness only works when lifestyle expectations align. Discuss these before signing.

Cleaning expectations

Who handles dishes, trash, and common areas? A shared chore chart prevents resentment when one person pays more but cleans less.

Guest policies

Overnight guests, partners staying often, and quiet hours affect everyone’s sleep and utility usage—set boundaries early.

Grocery sharing

Shared staples vs labeled shelves. Decide whether groceries are split equally, itemized, or mostly separate.

Bill payment habits

One person on the lease? Use a shared spreadsheet or app. Due dates, autopay, and reimbursement within 48 hours reduce conflict.

Roommate money mistakes to avoid

Most conflicts are preventable with clear agreements upfront.

Unequal utility usage

One person cranks heat or runs servers 24/7 while others subsidize. Track usage or switch to weighted splits.

Unclear rent splits

Verbal “we’ll figure it out” deals break when someone gets the master bedroom. Write percentages before move-in.

No shared expense tracking

Streaming, toilet paper, and cleaning supplies add up. Use a shared ledger or rotation system.

No emergency plan

If one roommate loses income, can others cover a month? Discuss backup plans before you need them.

Frequently asked questions

How should roommates split rent?

Equal split when rooms match. Bedroom-size split when one suite is larger. Income-based when salaries differ. Document the method and percentages before anyone moves in.

Is living with roommates cheaper?

Usually yes—you share rent, utilities, internet, and sometimes groceries. Savings vs a solo 1-bedroom often run 30–45% depending on city and roommate count.

How much money can roommates save?

In a $2,400 apartment split three ways, each person might pay $800–$1,150/month all-in versus $1,500–$2,100+ for a solo 1-bedroom in the same metro.

Should roommates split utilities equally?

Equal splits work when usage is similar. Heavy AC, WFH, or extra devices may warrant a utility-weighted or income-based split—decide upfront.

What salary is needed with roommates?

Often $45k–$55k gross per person in mid-cost cities when your share is ~$1,000–$1,200/month all-in. High-cost metros need more—use the planner with your city.
