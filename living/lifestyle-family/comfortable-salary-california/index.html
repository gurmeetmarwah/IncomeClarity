What does "comfortable" mean?

We use four levels. Pick the life you want. Your current setting: —

Basic survival

Bills get paid. Almost nothing saved. A car repair hurts. Fine for a short time. Not where you want to stay.

Stable

Bills are easy. You eat out sometimes. You save a little. A big bill stings. But it does not break you.

Comfortable

You save real money each month. You take a real vacation. Emergencies are covered. This is what most California families aim for.

Upper middle class

You live where you want. You drive what you want. You save a lot. Big choices do not need trade-offs.

It depends on who you are. $100,000 looks very different for a single renter in Sacramento than a family of four in San Francisco.

California housing costs

Housing is the biggest lever in any California budget. In LA or San Francisco, rent or a mortgage eats 35% to 50% of take-home pay for a family. Inland, it drops to about 25%.

What this means for you

Renters get more house for less. A $4,000 rent in LA is $48,000 a year. The mortgage on the same house is closer to $86,000 a year. That is a $38,000 jump. For the same four walls.

5 ways to lower your cost of living

Most California families use at least one of these. None are magic. All are real.

Move inland

Fresno is 50% cheaper than San Francisco. Sacramento is 30% cheaper than LA. A 2-hour drive can cut your rent in half.

Work remote

Keep your coastal salary. Pay inland rent. Just check your employer allows it. And check the tax rules.

Use public transit

One car instead of two saves $500 to $900 a month. This only works in SF, LA, and San Diego. And a few transit-rich pockets.

Cut childcare costs

Daycare in California runs $1,800 to $3,000 a month per kid. A nanny share or flex schedule can cut that in half.

Cook at home more

Eating out is the biggest budget leak in California. Meal prep on Sundays can save a family $400 to $700 a month.

Frequently asked questions

What salary is comfortable in California?

It depends on your city and family size. A single renter in Sacramento needs about $75,000. A family of four in San Diego needs about $165,000. In San Francisco, that same family needs $220,000.

Is $100k enough in California?

For one person or a couple with no kids, yes. Almost anywhere in the state. For a family of four, it works inland. But it feels tight in LA and San Diego. And it is not enough in the Bay Area.

What is the cheapest California city?

Fresno, Bakersfield, and Riverside have the lowest rent. Sacramento is a popular middle ground. You get California weather without the coastal price.

How much does a family of four need in California?

About $135,000 in Fresno. About $165,000 in San Diego. About $220,000 in San Francisco. Add 30% to 40% if you plan to buy a home.

Can one income support a family here?

Yes. But only if you live inland and rent. One earner can support a family of four on $150,000 in Fresno or Sacramento. The same income feels stretched in LA. And is not enough in the Bay Area.
