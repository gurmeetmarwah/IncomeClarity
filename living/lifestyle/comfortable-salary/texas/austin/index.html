What “comfortable” means in Austin

Comfort is not just paying rent on time. In our model, a comfortable Austin salary covers median housing, groceries, utilities, and transport — plus 10–12% savings and modest lifestyle spending (dining, hobbies, an emergency fund). A basic tier covers essentials with minimal savings. Comfortable plus adds travel, nicer housing, and bigger savings.

Austin sits at cost-of-living index 108 (US average = 100). That is cheaper than Denver or Seattle, but pricier than Houston or San Antonio. Tech wages rose with migration after 2020 — so a $75k offer feels different than it did five years ago. No Texas state income tax helps: $85k gross in Austin often feels like $95k+ in California after tax on the same lifestyle.

Before you compare offers, convert gross to take-home in our hourly-to-salary after-tax calculator. Then check whether rent fits the 28% rule — on $85k, that is about $1,983/month for housing, close to Austin’s median 1BR.

Rent vs buy impact

Owning often raises housing cost even after equity build.

Comfortable salary when renting: $85,000. When owning a similar home: $100,000 (+$15,000 gross). Property tax in Travis County runs high — owners budget 1.5%+ of value yearly on top of the mortgage.

What to know about Austin

Local rent, tax, and daily costs drive how much you need to earn in Austin.

  • Cost-of-living index 108 (US average = 100) captures rent, food, utilities, and transport pressure.
  • Many singles plan around $82,000 gross here before childcare, debt, and extra savings goals.
  • Tax context: No state income tax; property tax is high for owners

Singles renting a 1-bedroom often need about $85,000 gross for a comfortable budget with savings. Basic tier starts near $60,000. See $75k in Austin if your offer is below that.

A family of four with rent and childcare often lands near $145,000 gross for a comfortable tier. Basic family budgets start around $110,000. Use our family of 4 income guide for childcare detail.
