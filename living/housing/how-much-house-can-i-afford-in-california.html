How Much House Can You Afford in California in 2026?

California uses the same lender rules as every other state. The 28/36 rule. But the prices are bigger. State tax shrinks your take-home. And insurance is often huge. The result: a "normal" coastal home can eat a "normal" salary. This page turns the 28/36 rule into California dollars. With real numbers. Then we point you to rent vs buy in California for the deeper choice.

At a glance: A family earning $150,000 a year ($12,500 a month) has a 28% housing cap of about $3,500 a month. But an $850,000 coastal home at 6.5% with 20% down costs $4,300+ a month just for the mortgage. Before tax and insurance. That gap is why smart California buyers always check take-home pay and rent vs buy. Not just the bank's approval letter.

The 28% and 36% rules still apply. California just changes the price.

Loan officers use the same rules in every state. 28% of gross pay for your housing payment. 36% of gross pay for all debt. What changes in California is how little home those percents buy. In LA, the Bay Area, San Diego, and most job hubs. And how little is left after state tax takes its cut.

What this means for you

The bank may approve you for an $850k coastal home on a $150k salary. But your real monthly cap is $3,500. The home costs $4,300+ a month. That gap eats your savings.

Two ways out: buy inland for less. Or rent and invest the difference. Both can build wealth. Neither is wrong.

State tax, Prop 13, and insurance: why "approved" is not "easy"

California income tax shrinks your take-home pay vs no-tax states like Texas. Prop 13 caps how fast property tax can grow for long-time owners. But new buyers pay tax close to the price they paid. Not their neighbor's old bill. And homeowners insurance is now a huge line item in many cities. Skip it in your math and you end up house-rich and cash-poor.

More room

FAQ: California house affordability

How much house can I afford in California on $150k?

The 28% rule gives you a housing cap of $3,500 a month. That fits many inland homes if you have low debt. But it usually does not fit a coastal home once tax and insurance hit. Use your lender's pre-approval. Then check it against your real take-home pay.

What income do you need for an $800k house in California?

It depends on your down payment, rate, tax, insurance, and other debts. The mortgage alone on $800k with 20% down at 6.5% is about $4,000 a month. Before tax and insurance. Most lenders want enough gross income to keep your full payment near 28% of that pay.

Is it better to rent or buy in California?

It depends on how long you stay, the local home and rent prices, and how much risk you can take. Read rent vs buy in California after you size the payment here.

Does Prop 13 lower my payment?

It caps how fast your property tax can grow once you own. But you still pay tax based on the price you paid. Plus local charges. Not your neighbor's old bill.
