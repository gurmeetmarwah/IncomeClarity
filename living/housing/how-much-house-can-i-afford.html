How Much House Can You Really Afford in 2026?

On a $90,000 salary, your safe house payment is about $2,100 a month. That fits a home around $280,000 at today's rates. The bank may approve more. Your gut may want less. Here is the simple 28/36 rule. Plus real numbers for five common salaries.

At a glance: Earn $90,000 a year? Your monthly gross is $7,500. The 28% rule sets your housing cap at $2,100 a month. That's your full payment — mortgage, tax, and insurance. It buys you a $280,000 home with 20% down at 6.5%. Push the down payment up or the rate down and the price goes up too. National median home prices sit near $420,000 — so this 28% rule may not stretch in every city.

The 28% and 36% rules in plain English

These are the rules of thumb loan officers use. They are not laws. FHA, VA, and jumbo loans have other limits. But they help you understand "how did the bank get that number?"

~28% Housing rule: Your full payment (mortgage + tax + insurance) should be near 28% of your gross pay.
~36% Total debt rule: Your housing plus car, card, and student loan payments should be near 36% of your gross pay.
2.5–3× Quick check: Some buyers hear "2.5 to 3 times your salary" as a price cap. Useful when rates and debts are normal. Not when they're not.

Real examples: what 28% buys at four salaries

Take your yearly gross. Divide by 12 for monthly. Then take 28%. That's your housing cap. Not a pre-approval — just the start.

What this means for you

The 28% rule gives you a safe housing cap. But the bank may approve more. They look at your gross pay. You live on your take-home pay.

So always check two numbers. The bank's 28/36 cap. And your real after-tax budget. Pick the smaller one. Aim there. That's a payment you can sleep on.

Two levers that move your price: down payment and rate

More down or lower rate
  • Smaller loan. Lower mortgage payment.
  • 20% down skips PMI. That saves $100 to $300 a month.
Less down or higher rate
  • Bigger loan. Bigger payment. Same 28% buys less house.
  • Same salary now caps out at a lower home price.

Lender max vs the payment you can sleep on

Banks will approve you at the edge of the rules. Smart buyers aim 5% to 15% under that cap. That leaves room for daycare, travel, or a job change. If 28% feels tight on your real take-home pay, treat the bank's number as a ceiling. Not a target.

Frequently asked questions

How much house can I afford on my salary?

Start with your monthly gross pay. Apply the 28% housing rule and the 36% total debt rule. Then adjust for your down payment, rate, tax, and insurance. Your lender will set the real cap.

What is the 28/36 rule?

28% of your gross pay for housing. 36% of your gross pay for all debt. Simple numbers most buyers still hear today.

How many times my salary should my home be?

Most people say 2.5 to 3 times your gross pay. Useful as a quick check. But at today's high rates, lean on the 28/36 payment math instead.

Should I use gross or net pay?

The bank uses gross pay for its rules. Your real budget runs on take-home pay. Check both numbers before you sign.

Home Affordability Methodology, Data Sources & Editorial Standards

Last reviewed: May 26, 2026 · Reviewed by the Income Clarity editorial team · Read the full methodology

How We Calculate Affordability

  • The 28/36 rule: housing ≤ 28% gross; total debt service ≤ 36% gross.
  • Mortgage: 30-year fixed amortization formula; default rate 6.5% (Freddie Mac PMMS).
  • Down payment: 20% default (PMI threshold on conforming loans).
  • Property tax + insurance: state median effective rate × home value, plus 0.35% home value/year insurance.

Data Sources

  • Freddie Mac PMMS — weekly mortgage rate.
  • Tax Foundation — state property tax rates.
  • Consumer Financial Protection Bureau — mortgage disclosure rules.

What This Isn't

A mortgage pre-approval. Lenders also weigh credit score, employment history, reserves, and any non-traditional income.
