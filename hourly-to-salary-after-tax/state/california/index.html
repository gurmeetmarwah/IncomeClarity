Is $25 an hour enough in California?

$25 an hour pays about $3,460 a month after tax. In inland cities, that works. On the coast, it's paycheck-to-paycheck. Once rent, gas, and childcare hit.

What this means for you

$25 an hour in Fresno: you can save. $25 an hour in San Francisco: you barely break even on rent.

In California, your ZIP code matters as much as your wage. A 90-minute move inland can free up $1,000 a month. For the same job.

Housing

California rent is the highest in the US. Bay Area and SoCal coast rents take 40% or more of your take-home. Sacramento and Riverside are friendlier.

Taxes

California has state income tax up to 13.3%. Plus SDI. So your net pay is lower than in no-tax states. Like Texas or Florida.

Transportation

Commutes are long. SF and LA have transit. But most people still need a car. California gas is the most expensive in the US. Parking can run $200 a month.

City variation

$25 an hour stretches in Bakersfield, Fresno, or the Inland Empire. It struggles in SF, San Jose, or Santa Monica. Same wage. Very different life.

Why California take-home pay is lower

Three things shrink your California paycheck more than most states.

State income tax

California has its own income tax. On top of federal. Rates start at 1%. And climb to 13.3% for top earners. That alone takes 4% to 9% from a typical paycheck.

Brackets that climb

The more you earn, the higher the rate on each new dollar. A $200,000 salary in California pays a much bigger share to the state. Versus the same $200,000 in flat-tax Illinois.

SDI and payroll cuts

You also pay California SDI. About 1.1%. Plus Social Security, Medicare, and federal tax. Health and 401(k) can cut your take-home more.

California paycheck reality FAQ

Common questions on California withholding, SDI, and coastal vs inland costs.

Why is California take-home lower than Texas?

California has state income tax. Up to 13.3%. Plus SDI of about 1.1%. Texas has neither. On a $75,000 salary, the gap is about $5,000 a year. Or roughly $400 a month in your bank.

What is California SDI?

SDI is State Disability Insurance. A small payroll tax. About 1.1% of your wages. It funds disability pay and family leave. It comes out whether you use it or not.

Is $100,000 a good salary in California?

It depends on your city. $100,000 in Fresno or Sacramento gives you a strong middle-class life. In SF, San Jose, or Santa Monica, you may still feel pinched. Especially with kids.

How We Calculate CA Take-Home

  • Federal tax: IRS 2025 brackets + standard deduction.
  • FICA: 6.2% Social Security to $168,600 wage cap + 1.45% Medicare.
  • Progressive brackets: 1% – 13.3% (top bracket at $698,275+ single, 2025).
  • California SDI: 1.1% of wages, no wage cap as of 2024.
  • No local income tax in California (unlike NYC).

Data Sources

  • IRS Publication 15-T — federal withholding tables.
  • Social Security Administration — annual wage base.
  • California Franchise Tax Board — 2024 rate schedules.

What This Isn't

A CA tax return preparer. We don't model 401(k) pretax deductions, employer health premiums, HSA contributions, or itemized deductions. Real take-home is usually within $300/month of our estimate.
