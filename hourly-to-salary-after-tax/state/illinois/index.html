Is $25 an Hour Enough in Illinois?

$25/hour in Illinois leaves about $3,430 a month after tax. That works in most of the state. It feels tight in Chicago and some suburbs where housing and transit costs run high.

What this means for you

The Illinois flat tax is the easy part. The hard part is property tax. The state has some of the highest in the US — often $6,000 to $12,000 a year on a $300,000 home.

If you rent, the flat tax keeps your math simple. If you buy, the property tax bill may matter more than the state income tax line on your check.

Housing

Chicago is the priciest part of Illinois. Most of all near transit and jobs. Downstate cities like Peoria, Decatur, or Champaign offer lower rent.

Taxes

The 4.95% flat tax makes your paycheck simple. But county property tax can shape your real cost. It can even push up rent in some suburbs.

Transit

Chicago has CTA and Metra, which can cut car costs. Most suburban and downstate households still need a car. That adds gas and insurance to your monthly bills.

City matters

$25/hour stretches in Springfield, Bloomington, or Rockford. It is tighter on Chicago's North Side or in high-tax suburbs. County and school-district costs drive the gap.

How the Illinois Flat Tax Shapes Your Budget

Illinois is unlike most big states. The state tax is simple. But the property tax is where most people feel the cost.

One flat rate

Illinois charges 4.95% across all income levels. That makes it easy to model a raise, side gig, or bonus. No bracket math needed.

Easy to plan

The state rate does not climb with income. So most workers can forecast take-home with few surprises when their pay changes.

Property tax is the swing

For buyers, property tax matters more than the state tax line. A Chicago suburb may cost more in tax than a downstate home twice the size.

What Can You Afford in Illinois?

On a $70,000 salary, take-home is about $4,400 a month. In Chicago, many renters spend 30% to 40% of that on housing. Outside Chicago — Aurora, Joliet, Rockford, Peoria, or Champaign — the same income covers rent at 25% to 30% and leaves more room to save.

Illinois Flat-Tax and Affordability FAQ

Designed for Illinois readers comparing flat-tax paycheck math with county-level housing costs.

Is Illinois income tax flat or progressive?

Illinois uses a flat 4.95% state tax. The rate is the same at every income level. Unlike California or New York, it does not climb as you earn more.

Does Chicago have a city income tax?

No. Chicago does not tax wages directly. You still pay federal tax, FICA, and the Illinois state tax. Plus higher sales tax and property tax.

Why do Illinois housing costs vary so much by county?

County property tax rates, school districts, and local demand drive big monthly gaps. In many Illinois markets, the local property tax matters more than the paycheck tax line.

How much is $30 an hour after taxes in Illinois?

$30 an hour at 40 hours/week is about $62,400 gross. After federal tax, FICA, and the 4.95% state tax, take-home is around $4,080 a month. Use the calculator above for your real number.

How We Calculate IL Take-Home

  • Federal tax: IRS 2025 brackets + standard deduction.
  • FICA: 6.2% Social Security to $168,600 wage cap + 1.45% Medicare.
  • Flat state income tax: 4.95% on all wage income (no brackets).
  • No local wage tax in Chicago or other IL cities for residents.
  • Personal exemption: $2,775 per dependent (2024 schedule).

Data Sources

  • IRS Publication 15-T — federal withholding tables.
  • Social Security Administration — annual wage base.
  • Illinois Department of Revenue.

What This Isn't

A IL tax return preparer. We don't model 401(k) pretax deductions, employer health premiums, HSA contributions, or itemized deductions. Real take-home is usually within $300/month of our estimate.
