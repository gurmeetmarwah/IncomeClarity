Is $25 an Hour Enough in Texas?

$25/hour generally provides a comfortable lifestyle across most of Texas, especially given the absence of a state income tax. Affordability still varies sharply between metros and cities.

Housing

Texas housing costs range from very affordable in Lubbock or El Paso to sharply higher in Austin. Median asking rents in San Antonio and Houston usually fit a 30% rent rule at $25/hour better than Austin's hot rental market.

Taxes

Texas has no state income tax, so paycheck withholding is mostly federal + FICA. However, Texas property taxes are above the national average, which can shift the total tax picture if you own a home.

Transportation

Most Texas cities are car-dependent, so fuel, insurance, and maintenance are meaningful line items. Austin and Houston have growing transit options but coverage is limited compared with denser metros.

City variation

$25/hour stretches further in San Antonio, El Paso, or Lubbock than it does in Austin and parts of Dallas–Fort Worth. Affordability narratives in Texas hinge on the specific metro you live in.

Why Texas Take-Home Pay Is Higher

For the same gross salary, Texas paychecks usually clear more cash than paychecks in income-tax states. Three reasons why.

No Texas state income tax

Texas does not collect a personal state income tax. There's no progressive bracket eating into your after-tax salary the way California or New York's brackets do.

Federal + FICA only on wages

Wage withholding is essentially federal income tax plus Social Security and Medicare. That keeps take-home income closer to gross than in states with their own income brackets layered on top.

Property & sales taxes elsewhere

Texas funds services partly through higher property and sales taxes. These don't reduce your paycheck directly, but they should be considered when comparing total tax burden city by city.

Texas Salary & Tax FAQ

Do you pay state income tax in Texas?

No. Texas does not levy a state income tax on individuals. Wage withholding only covers federal income tax and FICA (Social Security + Medicare), which is why take-home income in Texas runs higher than in states like California or New York at the same gross pay.

Is Texas a tax-friendly state?

For paychecks, yes—Texas is among the most tax-friendly states because there is no state income tax. Total tax burden depends on property and sales taxes too, so the picture can shift if you own a home or spend heavily on taxable goods.

What is a good salary in Texas?

A good salary in Texas depends on city and family size. Austin generally requires more household income than Houston, Dallas–Fort Worth, or San Antonio because of housing costs. Use the calculator above to translate your hourly wage into after-tax salary for your specific situation.

How much is $30/hour after taxes in Texas?

At $30/hour × 40 hours/week, gross pay is about $62,400/year. With no Texas state income tax, take-home is roughly $4,340/month after federal tax and FICA. Run the calculator above for a more personalized projection.
