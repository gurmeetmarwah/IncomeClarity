Is $25 an Hour Enough in Washington?

$25/hour in Washington leaves about $3,650 a month after tax. That feels solid in most cities. Seattle and the Eastside take most of the win through higher rent, childcare, and daily costs.

What this means for you

A one-bedroom rent in Seattle can be $2,200 a month. In Spokane, the same kind of place is closer to $1,200. That gap is $12,000 a year on rent alone — much bigger than the state-tax saving.

So your Washington win is real, but it depends on the ZIP code. East of the Cascades, the no-tax math pays off. In Seattle, the rent floor eats most of it.

Housing

Washington is split. Seattle and Bellevue are top-tier. Spokane, Tacoma, and Yakima are far cheaper. The gap is wide for the same wage.

Taxes

No state income tax helps net pay. But high sales tax in many Puget Sound cities (close to 10%) means daily costs are still high.

Transit

Seattle's transit is growing and can cut car costs in core areas. Most suburbs and eastern Washington still need a car for most trips.

City matters

$25/hour stretches much more east of the Cascades than in Seattle ZIPs. The state offers a choice: top tech wages or lower cost of living.

Why Washington Paychecks Vary So Much by Region

Washington often shows strong take-home pay. But your real budget depends on where you live in the state.

No state tax on wages

Washington has no state tax on wages. That keeps your direct paycheck cuts lower than in high-tax states.

Seattle has a high cost floor

Even with strong net pay, Seattle rent and services set a high baseline. So you may feel less of a tax win than your paycheck shows.

State tax falls on spending

Washington taxes sales and business more than wages. So you feel the state mostly in what you buy, not in your paycheck.

Washington Net Pay FAQ

Covers no-income-tax wages, WA payroll premiums, and the Seattle cost-of-living reality.

Does Washington tax my paycheck?

No. Washington has no state tax on wages. Your paycheck is mostly federal tax and FICA — same as Texas or Florida.

What are WA Cares and Paid Family Medical Leave fees?

They are small state program fees that may show up on your check. They are not income tax. Each one is well under 1% of your pay.

Why does Seattle still feel pricey if there is no state tax?

Because no state tax does not lower rent or grocery costs. Seattle rent, childcare, and high sales tax all keep your daily budget tight.

How much is $30 an hour after taxes in Washington?

$30 an hour at 40 hours/week is about $62,400 gross. With no state tax, take-home is around $4,340 a month after federal tax and FICA. Use the calculator above to plug in your own wage.

How We Calculate WA Take-Home

  • Federal tax: IRS 2025 brackets + standard deduction.
  • FICA: 6.2% Social Security to $168,600 wage cap + 1.45% Medicare.
  • No state income tax on wages — only the federal government and FICA touch your wages.
  • WA Cares Fund: 0.58% payroll tax on wages (long-term care, optional opt-out before 2024).
  • WA Paid Family & Medical Leave: ~0.74% employee share of wages.

Data Sources

  • IRS Publication 15-T — federal withholding tables.
  • Social Security Administration — annual wage base.
  • Washington State Department of Revenue.

What This Isn't

A WA tax return preparer. We don't model 401(k) pretax deductions, employer health premiums, HSA contributions, or itemized deductions. Real take-home is usually within $300/month of our estimate.
