Is $25 an Hour Enough in Washington?

$25/hour generally provides a comfortable lifestyle outside the Seattle metro thanks to no state income tax, but Seattle and the Eastside often push the same wage to the edge of comfort due to rent and home prices.

Housing

Seattle and Bellevue carry some of the highest housing costs in the US, while Spokane, Tacoma, Olympia, and the Tri-Cities are dramatically more affordable. The same Washington wage produces very different lifestyles by region.

Taxes

No state income tax keeps paycheck withholding limited to federal + FICA. Sales tax is on the higher side (6.5% state plus local) and Seattle's combined sales tax often runs near 10%, which affects spending more than savings.

Transportation

Seattle has growing transit (Link light rail, buses, ferries), so car ownership is optional in some neighborhoods. Most of the rest of Washington is car-dependent with the usual fuel and insurance costs.

City variation

$25/hour stretches further in Spokane, Yakima, or the Tri-Cities than it does in Seattle, Bellevue, or Redmond. Washington affordability narratives depend heavily on whether you're east or west of the Cascades.

Why Washington Take-Home Pay Is Higher

For the same gross salary, Washington paychecks usually clear more cash than paychecks in income-tax states. Three reasons why.

No Washington state income tax

Washington does not collect a personal state income tax on wages. There's no progressive bracket eating into your after-tax salary the way California or New York's brackets do.

Federal + FICA only on wages

Wage withholding is essentially federal income tax plus Social Security and Medicare. That keeps take-home income closer to gross than in states with their own income brackets layered on top.

Sales & B&O taxes elsewhere

Washington funds services partly through sales tax (one of the higher rates in the US in some metros) and a Business & Occupation tax on businesses. These don't reduce your paycheck directly but shift the total tax picture.

Washington Salary & Tax FAQ

Does Washington have a state income tax?

No. Washington does not levy a personal state income tax on wages. Wage withholding only covers federal income tax and FICA (Social Security + Medicare), which is why take-home income in Washington runs higher than in states like California or New York at the same gross pay.

Is Washington a tax-friendly state for paychecks?

For wage earners, yes—Washington is among the most paycheck-friendly states. Sales tax is relatively high in some metros, and small payroll premiums for Paid Family Medical Leave and WA Cares apply, but income tax on wages is zero.

What is a good salary in Washington?

A good salary in Washington depends heavily on city. Seattle and Bellevue generally require more household income than Spokane, Tacoma, or the Tri-Cities because of housing costs. Use the calculator above to translate your hourly wage into after-tax salary for your specific situation.

How much is $30/hour after taxes in Washington?

At $30/hour × 40 hours/week, gross pay is about $62,400/year. With no Washington state income tax, take-home is roughly $4,340/month after federal tax and FICA. Run the calculator above for a more personalized projection.
