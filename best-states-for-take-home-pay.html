Best states for take-home pay

Take-home pay is what remains on your paycheck after taxes and payroll deductions. Among US states, the biggest swing in net wages—holding gross income equal—usually comes from whether your state taxes wages. This guide explains which states often rank strongest for paycheck take-home, important caveats, and where to run numbers next.

What “best for take-home” means

Most employees pay federal income tax withholding, FICA (Social Security and Medicare), and—where applicable—state and local withholdings. States that do not tax wages at the state level remove one large slice from that stack, so identical gross pay often produces a larger deposit than in a high-tax state. “Best” here is about paycheck mechanics, not total wealth or lifestyle—two people with the same gross wage can still have very different budgets once housing and prices enter the picture.

States often cited for higher net paychecks

These states generally do not levy a broad statewide income tax on wages in the same way many states do (rules change; verify current law for your situation):

  • Alaska
  • Florida
  • Nevada
  • New Hampshire (no general tax on most earned wages; confirm other income types separately)
  • South Dakota
  • Tennessee
  • Texas
  • Washington
  • Wyoming

Each state still has other policies—sales taxes, property taxes, insurance mandates, and special payroll assessments—that affect your overall budget. Washington, for example, layers other wage-related programs over time; Alaska has unique cost-of-living realities despite favorable tax positioning for many workers.

Rent, sales tax, and local withholdings

Cost of living can dwarf tax differences. A higher take-home paycheck in a no-income-tax state might still feel tight if rent consumes 40–50% of net pay. Some cities impose local income or payroll-related taxes (for example, large metros in the Northeast and Midwest), which can reduce take-home even when the state itself is mid-tier or has no state income tax structure similar to California’s.

If you are budgeting a move, combine a state take-home estimate with a housing and transportation thumbnail before you treat a tax map as the final word.

Frequently asked questions

Which US states are best for take-home pay?

At the same gross wage, states without a broad state income tax on wages typically show larger net paychecks than high state-tax states because state withholding can be sizable. Exact ordering depends on your income level, deductions, and local taxes.

Is a no-income-tax state always the smartest move?

No. A higher paycheck can be undone by housing, childcare, commuting, insurance, or regressive consumption taxes depending on where you live. Model take-home alongside rent or mortgage assumptions.

Do federal taxes still apply in no-income-tax states?

Yes. Federal income tax withholding and FICA still apply—take-home never equals gross anywhere in the US for typical employees.
