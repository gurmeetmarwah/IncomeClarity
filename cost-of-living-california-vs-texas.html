Cost of living: California vs Texas

People compare these two states constantly—but “cheaper” depends on your metro, your rent, and your tax return, not a slogan. This page gives a simple picture of how state income tax and housing dollars usually behave, a same-offer thought experiment, and one-click paths to Income Clarity’s California and Texas after-tax tools. Nothing here replaces your real lease quotes or paystub.

At a glance: On the same gross salary, Texas often wins take-home because it has no state income tax. California can still “win” lifestyle for you if the job, family network, or neighborhood fit matters—or if Texas housing in your target city has caught up faster than expected. The tie-breaker is almost always rent or mortgage for the address you would actually choose, not the shape of the state on a map.

Housing: the other giant lever

State tax math is clean; rent is emotional and local. A Texas offer can look amazing on a paystub and still feel tight if you insist on a specific school zone or new-build suburb. A California offer can feel scary on paper and work if you already own a home with a manageable payment.

Think “metro,” not “state outline”

California includes both ultra-expensive coastal markets and lower-cost inland cities. Texas includes both relatively affordable towns and fast-growing metros where rents rose quickly. That is why we pair this article with how much rent can I afford and the rent vs buy calculator—your housing line should come from listings you would actually sign.

Example: “Same $95,000 offer” — how to think about it

Riley gets the same $95,000 remote-eligible offer and can live in either state. Riley is not you, but the order of operations is useful:

  3. 3

Frequently asked questions

Is Texas cheaper than California?

Often yes on take-home for the same gross because of no state income tax, and yes on housing in many comparisons between coastal California and large Texas metros—but not always. Model your offer and your actual rent or mortgage.

Does Texas have state income tax?

Texas does not levy state personal income tax on wages the way California does. You still pay federal tax and other costs of living.

Why can California still feel expensive with a high salary?

State taxes and high rent in major job hubs can consume a large share of even strong gross pay, leaving less slack than people expect.

How do I compare the same offer in both states?

Use after-tax tools for each state, subtract real housing and insurance, and compare what remains for savings and discretionary spending.
