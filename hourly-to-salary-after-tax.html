“$15, $20, $30, $40 an hour is how much a year?"

If you are searching for an hourly to salary calculator mindset without doing math by hand, start by converting your hourly wage to annual salary using the standard full-time assumption: multiply your hourly rate by weekly hours, then multiply by 52 weeks. That gross figure is what employers often annualize from an hourly job posting. To convert hourly to yearly salary into something you can budget, you still need after-tax cash flow—which changes by filing status, deductions, and state. Our calculator above turns your inputs into estimated take-home pay by state. $15/hour is how much a year?

$15/hour = $31,200/year

Before tax · full-time (40 hrs/week × 52 weeks)

After tax (US average): ~$2,050/month

Details

Gross annual salary (before taxes)

To convert hourly wage to annual salary at $15/hour on a 40-hour week, use $15 × 40 × 52 = $31,200 per year gross. That is the headline number job seekers compare when asking “how much a year” from an hourly rate.

Hourly to monthly salary (gross)

For a quick hourly to monthly salary view, divide the annual amount by 12: about $2,600/month gross. Biweekly pay is common in the US; with 26 pay periods, each check is roughly half of your monthly gross before deductions, depending on how payroll is configured.

After taxes and real-world budgeting

Take-home pay is lower once federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and state taxes apply. The ~$2,050/month after-tax figure shown here is an approximate national-average illustration—your state can move this materially. Who this level often describes

Many entry-level hourly roles cluster near this band. Housing costs and local prices matter more than the gross stat alone, which is why pairing this benchmark with an actual estimate beats relying on a single national average.

Gross annual salary (before taxes)

Taxes and benefits

Higher gross pay can push more income through marginal tax brackets, so average tax rates may rise compared with lower hourly tiers. Pretax benefits like health premiums or 401(k) contributions also change net pay even when gross stays constant.

Lifestyle band (high level)

In many regions, this gross level supports a middle-income household budget if housing stays within typical guidelines—but coastal rents or large debt loads can still squeeze cash flow, which is why after-tax estimates matter.

Popular Income Questions (FAQ)

What is an hourly to salary calculator?

An hourly to salary calculator converts your pay rate into yearly and monthly estimates—usually gross pay first, then take-home after taxes. Instead of manually multiplying hours by weeks, you enter your hourly rate, typical weekly hours, and location so federal and state withholding assumptions better match US paycheck patterns. It is most useful when comparing job offers, raises stated “per hour,” or freelance hourly quotes that you want to compare with salaried roles.

How do I convert hourly wage to annual salary?

To convert hourly wage to annual salary, multiply your hourly pay by the hours you work each week, then multiply by 52 weeks: annual gross ≈ hourly × weekly hours × 52. Full-time is often modeled as 40 hours per week. If your schedule varies, use your realistic average weekly hours so the annual figure reflects overtime or part-time work accurately.

How do I convert hourly to monthly salary?

To convert hourly to monthly salary on a gross basis, first compute annual gross pay (hourly × weekly hours × 52), then divide by 12. That yields calendar-month gross income. If you are budgeting from paychecks, remember biweekly pay does not map cleanly onto calendar months—some months have three biweekly checks—which is why monthly gross from annualization is a planning shortcut, not always identical to cash hitting your account each calendar month.

What is the formula to convert hourly to yearly salary?

The common US formula to convert hourly to yearly salary before taxes is: hourly rate × hours per week × 52 weeks. For example, $24/hour at 40 hours/week is $24 × 40 × 52 = $49,920 gross per year. After-tax income requires estimating withholdings—use the calculator on this page with your state for a closer net-pay figure.

Is $25/hour good in the US?

$25/hour can be a healthy wage in lower-cost regions and tighter for high-rent metros once taxes and benefits are considered. “Good” depends on household size, debt, childcare, and savings goals—not only the gross rate. Use the tool above to estimate take-home pay in your state, then compare rent and fixed bills to see what remains for savings and discretionary spending.

How much rent can you afford on $25/hour?

A common rule of thumb is to target rent near 30% of monthly take-home pay, though real budgets often require trade-offs. Because taxes vary by state, two people earning $25/hour can afford different rent ceilings. Estimate net monthly income first with this page’s hourly to salary calculator, then multiply by 0.30 as a starting point and adjust for debt payments and savings targets.

$30/hour is how much per year?

At 40 hours per week, $30/hour equals about $62,400 gross per year before taxes ($30 × 40 × 52). Monthly gross is roughly $5,200 (annual ÷ 12). Net pay depends on withholding and state taxes—run your scenario above for an estimated after-tax monthly figure tailored to your location.

How much do taxes take from hourly pay?

Most US employees see federal income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare (FICA), and often state income tax. Effective rates vary by income level and deductions; higher hourly rates may face higher marginal federal rates. Self-employed workers have different rules (for example, self-employment tax), which is why W-2 hourly modeling differs from 1099-style income—use specialized tools if you are contractor-paid.
